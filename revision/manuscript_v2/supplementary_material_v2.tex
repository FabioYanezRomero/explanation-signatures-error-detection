\subsection*{SubgraphX: explanation and node ranking}
SubgraphX returns a set of nodes rather than a ranking. The explanation evaluated in every metric is the highest-scoring coalition within the node budget (20\% of the nodes) returned by the Monte Carlo tree search. The progression metrics of Dimensions~1 and~2 require an importance ranking over the elements of the explanation; for SubgraphX this ranking is the \emph{search credit} of each node, defined as the sum, over all coalitions explored by the search, of the coalition score divided by the coalition size, and it is truncated to the coalition, so that the deletion and insertion trajectories span the same 20\% budget as for the other explainers.

\subsection*{Text-to-graph conversion: parser models}
Hierarchical graphs are extracted with Stanza. POS tagging and dependency parsing use the \texttt{combined\_charlm} model, which incorporates character-level language model features; lemmatisation uses \texttt{combined\_nocharlm}; constituency parsing uses \texttt{ptb3-revised\_charlm}, trained on Penn Treebank 3 (revised), and produces Penn Treebank-style phrase structures. Dependency parsing yields head--dependent relations with morphological annotations (POS tags, lemmas, morphological features).

\section{Coefficients of the combined detector}

Table~\ref{tab:coef} gives the standardised coefficients of the explanation features in the combined detector (confidence plus explanation, Equation~1 of the main text), one column per configuration and one block per dataset. The confidence features (maximum softmax probability, margin, entropy) are in the model but not shown. \(^{*}\): the 95\% interval from 200 bootstrap refits excludes zero.

How to read the table: every feature is standardised, so magnitudes are comparable within a column, and the detector predicts that a prediction is correct, so a positive coefficient means that a larger value of the feature makes the detector more inclined to declare the prediction correct and a negative one that it points towards an error.

Because the confidence features are in the same model, a metric that only repeats the confidence receives a small coefficient whose sign may change from one topology to the next; a metric that carries information of its own keeps a coefficient of the same sign across topologies. This is the reading behind the paragraph ``Dimension weights'' of Section~4.3 of the main text.

\section{Error detection with respect to the teacher's labels}

For the surrogates, an ``error'' can also be defined as a disagreement with the teacher, the target they were trained on. Table~\ref{tab:teacher} repeats for this target the detectors of Table~S12 (panel a) and the calibration and high-confidence analysis of Table~S13 (panel b).

The language model has no teacher and therefore no row here; its gold-label detection is in Table~S12. Disagreement with the teacher is a different and easier task than a gold-label error, since the surrogate is well calibrated with respect to the labels it was trained on and its confidence alone detects the disagreements (AUROC 0.96--0.98), so the values of this table are not comparable with those of Table~S12 or Figure~5 of the main text.

For reference, on the gold-label target the combined detector of the same surrogates reaches AUROC 0.83--0.86 on AG News and 0.80--0.84 on SST-2, and the language model's confidence 0.887 and 0.835 (Table~S12).

Table~\ref{tab:gbm} reports a gradient-boosting detector on the same features (gold-label errors). The logistic detector of Equation~1 of the main text is linear in the features; a gradient-boosting classifier trained on the same features and folds can exploit interactions and non-monotonic effects, so if the explanation carried information that a linear detector cannot use, the gain would appear here.

It does not change the picture: the gains of adding the explanation to the surrogate's confidence are of the same size and sign as with the logistic detector (AG News +0.011 to +0.019, SST-2 \(-0.027\) to \(-0.005\)), and for BERT the explanation still adds nothing (\(-0.004\) and \(-0.014\)); no surrogate reaches BERT's confidence with either detector.

\section{Overlap between graph and token explanations}

Table~\ref{tab:overlap} compares, instance by instance, the words that the graph explainer and TokenSHAP place inside the 20\% budget (for the graph explainers, the words of the selected nodes). Jaccard: size of the intersection over the size of the union of the two word sets. Spearman: rank correlation of the shared words, computed only when at least three words are shared. Top-1 match: share of instances whose highest-ranked word is the same in both.

Two selections of the same size drawn independently from the same text give an expected Jaccard of about 0.11 whatever the length of the text; the AG News values sit at that level and the SST-2 values slightly above it, which is what the granularity of short texts produces: an SST-2 explanation holds a median of four words (AG News: nine), so a single shared word already gives a Jaccard of 0.14--0.33.

The last three columns test whether the overlap says anything about correctness: the Jaccard index used alone as a score for BERT's errors gives AUROC 0.49--0.58 (0.5 is chance), and adding it to the nested detector of BERT's errors or of the surrogate's errors lowers the AUROC in every configuration. The two explainers therefore do not select the same words, and how much they agree does not indicate whether either model is right.

\section{Dimension 2: progression of the confidence drop}

Figure~\ref{fig:progression_combined} shows the two readings of Dimension~2 as curves over \(k\). Each point is the mean, over the instances of one explainer pooled across its four graph types (or over the language model for TokenSHAP), of the confidence lost when the \(k\) top-ranked elements are removed, \(c(x)-c(x_{\setminus S_k})\) (a), or when only those \(k\) elements are kept, \(c(x)-c(x_{S_k})\) (b); \(k\) is clipped to the size of the explanation. Solid lines are correct predictions and dashed lines errors.

In (a) the curve of the errors lies above the curve of the correct predictions for every explainer: removing the top-ranked elements costs more confidence on an error, which is the signal that the mask-out drop carries. The absolute drops are small (below 0.1) because 20\% of the input rarely changes a confident decision (Dimension~4). For TokenSHAP on SST-2 the error curve peaks at \(k=3\) and declines afterwards, because on BERT's errors removing more words raises the confidence as often as it lowers it (Section~4.2 of the main text); the signed mean cancels while the magnitude of the change keeps separating errors.

In (b) the order is largely reversed, correct predictions lose more than errors (the two TokenSHAP curves on SST-2 cross at \(k=5\)), because the drop is measured from the original confidence and correct predictions start from a higher one; this is why the sufficiency drop describes the explainer (GraphSVX explanations reproduce far more of the confidence than SubgraphX explanations) rather than the outcome, and is the weaker error signal of the two (Table~\ref{tab:single_feature}).

\section{Dimension 3 read as decisions}

Table~\ref{tab:margin_flips} gives the values plotted in Figure~4 of the main text together with the complementary reading. Flip: percentage of instances whose decision changes when the explanation is removed, that is, \(m(x_{\setminus S})<0\). Kept: percentage whose decision is reproduced by the explanation alone, \(m(x_S)>0\). Both are given separately for correct predictions and for errors. The two columns come from two different perturbations of the same instance, removing the explanation and keeping only the explanation, so they need not sum to 100: the complement of a flip is a decision that survives the removal, and the complement of a kept decision is an explanation that does not reproduce it on its own.

The error signal is in the flip columns: an error flips far more often than a correct prediction, for every explainer and topology.

The kept columns describe the explainer rather than the outcome: GraphSVX explanations keep the decision in 99--100\% of the correct predictions and 88--100\% of the errors, SubgraphX in 61--99\% and 49--93\% depending on the topology, TokenSHAP in 65--77\% and 55--59\%; the difference between correct and error is small next to the difference between explainers, which is why \(m(x_S)\) is a weak error signal in Table~\ref{tab:single_feature}.

\section{Single explanation metrics as error scores}

Table~\ref{tab:single_feature} asks how well each metric separates errors from correct predictions when used alone, without any detector. Each entry is the AUROC of the metric as an error score, oriented so that the more informative direction counts (the larger of AUROC and \(1-\)AUROC), so 0.5 is chance and no entry falls below it.

The first column is the maximum softmax probability of the explained model, the reference that every explanation metric has to be compared with; the remaining columns are the two metrics of each dimension.

The metrics that measure what remains after the explanation is removed (deletion AUC, mask-out drop, \(m(x_{\setminus S})\), \(F^{-}\)) approach the reference for every explainer, and the metrics that describe the explanation on its own (insertion AUC, sufficiency drop, \(m(x_S)\), \(F^{+}\)) stay below it and vary between explainers; the signed mask-out drop of TokenSHAP is at chance for the reason given in Section~4.2 of the main text.

Several explanation metrics separate errors better for the surrogates than the same metric does for the language model (on AG News, the mask-out drop and \(F^{-}\) reach 0.74--0.84 for the surrogates against 0.51 and 0.53 for TokenSHAP). This is not an advantage of the graph representation: for a surrogate every removal lowers the confidence, more on its errors, so these metrics carry the surrogate's confidence, whereas for BERT the drop changes sign and cancels. The comparison that matters is each metric against the confidence of its own model, first column, and on that comparison no explanation metric of any model improves on it, while the confidence of the language model (0.889 on AG News, 0.848 on SST-2) stays at or above every metric of every surrogate. Nor do the explanation metrics, taken together, separate errors better for the surrogates: the detector trained on the four dimensions alone (Table~\ref{tab:error_detection_full}, ``Detector trained on the explanation'') reaches 0.82--0.86 for the eight graph configurations of AG News against 0.864 for TokenSHAP, the best of the nine, and 0.80--0.82 against 0.81 on SST-2, where the differences lie within the bootstrap intervals.

\section{Calibration and the high-confidence regime: both datasets}

Table~\ref{tab:calibration_full} gives Table~4 of the main text for both datasets, with the paired-bootstrap intervals of the gains of adding the explanation features to the confidence-only detector among the predictions with confidence above 0.9. On SST-2 each regime holds 38--43 errors. The lower block repeats the analysis with pre-trained node features.

ECE, the expected calibration error, measures how far a model's confidence is from its actual accuracy: the predictions are grouped into ten equal-width confidence bins, the mean confidence of each bin is compared with the accuracy observed in it, and ECE is the average of the absolute differences weighted by the size of the bins. A value of 0.04 means that a model which announces a confidence of 0.95 is right about 91\% of the time; 0.01 is a nearly perfectly calibrated model. Read with the next columns, the table says where the gap between surrogate and language model comes from: on AG News BERT has ECE 0.010 and is confident (above 0.9) in 31\% of its errors, whereas the surrogates, with the same number of errors, have ECE 0.041--0.044 and are confident in 56--61\% of theirs; on SST-2 BERT itself is less well calibrated (0.039, confident in 44\% of its errors) and the surrogates (0.049--0.056) are close to it. With respect to the teacher's labels the same surrogates have ECE 0.003--0.006 (Table~4 of the main text): their confidence measures agreement with the teacher, not correctness, and the errors that the teacher makes hesitantly they make with confidence. This is the confidence that the explanation partly recovers in the AG News rows, and that it cannot improve on SST-2.
